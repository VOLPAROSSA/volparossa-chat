Messaging through a shared network

Your message. Another way to reach them.

In development

Signal encrypts the message for the intended device. VOLPAROSSA is being developed to carry that encrypted message through a network of participating peers.

  1. 1

    Encrypt on your device

    Signal encrypts the message before it leaves your device.

  2. 2

    Travel through a shared network

    Participating peers carry encrypted envelopes. They do not receive your readable conversation or Signal session keys.

  3. 3

    Open on their device

    The intended device decrypts the message within the Signal conversation.

Eight equal peers, several cross-links and two possible routes connect the conversation partners. No single peer is a required hub. Crossing lines connect only at marked nodes; this illustration is not live network status.

What still needs to be connected

Decentralised message delivery is still in development. Registration, device linking and key discovery remain separate dependencies. Delivery must account for recipients’ devices and the sender’s linked devices.

Recovery

Lose a device.
Keep the conversation.

Recovery in development

Preserve an encrypted copy of your conversations and attachments, then recover it using available storage peers and your own credentials.

  1. 1

    Create an encrypted backup

    Signal exports a snapshot. The connector protects its private container before Core receives the archive.

  2. 2

    Distribute protected fragments

    Core places multiple copies of encrypted fragments across selected storage participants.

  3. 3

    Retrieve and verify

    Available peers supply the fragments needed to reconstruct and check the encrypted archive.

  4. 4

    Restore with your credentials

    Decrypt and import through Signal’s recovery flow using the required owner-held recovery material.

{{archive_icon}}

Demonstrated in a scoped desktop trial

An encrypted Signal backup was recovered and imported after one of three storage providers went offline. Restored messages and attachments were checked.

The three providers were isolated environments on one test machine. This does not establish availability across independent hardware.

What you need for recovery

Keep Signal’s backup/account recovery material, the connector’s private wrapper descriptor and the signed reconstruction records with your owner identity. The wrapper key alone cannot locate or restore a backup. Usable credential-management tools are still in development.

Reciprocal storage

Make room
for each other.

Contribution management in development

The storage model is reciprocal: contribute capacity in proportion to the space your backups occupy across the network.

{{storage_picture}}
{{exchange_icon}}

1 GB backup × 2 copies
≈ 2 GB contribution+ overhead

The contribution target includes recovery copies and storage overhead. It should decrease when your actual remote usage decreases. Other people’s live fragments must move safely before their occupied space is released.

Automatic contribution management is still being developed.

Visible delivery routes

The intended interface distinguishes VOLPAROSSA delivery, mixed delivery and ordinary Signal delivery, including linked devices.

{{key_icon}}

Owner-held recovery credentials

Keep an independent, private copy of all credentials needed for recovery. Encrypted fragments alone cannot restore a conversation.

Private messages, calls and backups stay outside the public cache and shared-brain training.

Compatibility and your delivery choices

Ordinary Signal compatibility remains part of the design. A strict VOLPAROSSA-only delivery choice must not silently fall back to Signal delivery when the required route is unavailable.
